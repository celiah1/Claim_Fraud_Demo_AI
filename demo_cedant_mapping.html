EXPLAINER · AI use case #8 "Learn a cedant's spreadsheet layout" · made-up data · the AI step is simulated

What is "learning a cedant's spreadsheet layout"?

A reinsurer receives claims spreadsheets (called bordereaux) from many insurers (called cedants). Every cedant sends a different layout. Before anyone can compare them, someone has to work out which column means what. AI can do that step.

🏢CedantsInsurers in HK, Singapore and mainland China pass part of their risk to the reinsurer
📊BordereauxEach month, every cedant sends a claims spreadsheet in its own format
😩The painAnalysts spend hours re-mapping columns by hand before any analysis
✨With AIAI suggests the mapping once, a person approves it, and it's saved for every later month

1Three cedants, three different spreadsheets

Same kind of data, but click each one: different column names, languages, date formats and currencies.

2AI suggests what each column means AI

For each column, the AI reads the column name and a few sample values, then suggests the standard field, a confidence level and its reason. A person approves or corrects it.

3One clean, comparable table

All three cedants in the same columns, with dates in one format and every amount converted to USD. Now the screening rules can compare them.

💾
3 templates saved. Next month, when Cedant A sends its sheet, ClaimGuard recognises the layout and maps it instantly, with no AI call and no human work. AI is only used again if a cedant changes its layout.

Why a reinsurer cares

❌ Today (manual)• An analyst re-maps each cedant's sheet in Excel
• Hours per cedant per month; errors creep in
• New cedants take days to onboard
• Cedants are hard to compare, so leakage stays hidden
✅ With AI mapping• Minutes to map a new cedant, approved by a person
• Saved templates make later months automatic
• Every cedant in one comparable table
• Screening rules and dashboards work across all cedants

Why it's rated easy to deploy: the AI only needs the column names plus 3–5 sample rows, which can be anonymised first, not the whole claims file. It's a one-off step per cedant, so the cost is tiny.
